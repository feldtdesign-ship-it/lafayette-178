The building

A 1906 walk-up

Cream brick, a red fire escape down the front, three windows a floor, six floors. The laundromat at 176 on one side, the red cast-iron front of 180 on the other. The shop is the whole ground floor but for the apartment door beside it.

The space
705square feet on the ground, plus the cellar

One clean room

Eighteen feet two inches wide, twenty-five deep, under a white pressed-tin ceiling with ten bare bulbs. Pale grey tile, white walls. Behind the room a bath and the stair down to the cellar for stock. The storefront is one run of black steel: a display window, a pair of glass doors, a second display window, vents above and a plain black fascia ready for one name.

The store

Prints, tees, the figure

The south wall is the gallery: his line drawings of New York in one level row of black frames. The north wall is matte black pipe and white oak plank, folded tees above, a rail of hangers below, one colourway to a bay. Two oak tables carry the drop. Gumbit stands four feet tall on a black plinth in the north window, facing the street. A Galaga cabinet sits kitty-corner at the back.

The work

Drawn by hand, printed in New York

Graphic tees on a 7.5 ounce USA-cotton blank in cream, navy, lilac, moss and peach, crop tees, hoodies and bombers. Art prints in two sizes. Gumbit, the bubblegum-pink suit-and-tie character, going from the drawings to a small collectible edition. Kids sizes already in the line. Everything carries the signature.

The business

Fourteen years underground and up

On September 26, 2012, PJ O'Rourke II wheeled a hand-built art cart onto an MTA subway platform and started selling his prints to whoever walked past. No connections, no capital, no permission. Eight years underground, then pop-ups in DUMBO from 2014, the prints becoming apparel on a used heat press, and in 2019 the first storefront in the East Village at 11th Street and First Avenue.

Why 178 Lafayette

Small, bright, on the right street

Lafayette is the seam between the cast-iron district and the little streets east of it; the SoHo shopper walks east, the Nolita local walks west. A 700 square foot flagship on a corridor people already walk is the format PJ has proved for a decade: tight, curated, the stock downstairs and the window doing the work. The room needs almost nothing done to it; the fit-out is freestanding. In and trading fast.
